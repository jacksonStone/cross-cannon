# Logos daily-use feature research

Research date: 2026-11-12. Scope: ordinary daily reading, personal study, and small-group preparation. Sources are Logos product/support documentation; this is a documented capability inventory, not a hands-on usability review. Availability depends on platform, library ownership, subscription, and legacy licenses. No current price comparison is attempted.

## Platforms, content, and licensing boundaries

- Desktop is the fullest workspace. Mobile and web support core search, reading, comparison, notebooks, guides, and multiple resources, but feature parity is incomplete. The official matrix lists richer desktop notes/highlights, original-language tools, printing/export, and workflows; its availability chart is not a promise that every user owns every feature. [Platform Comparison](https://support.logos.com/hc/en-us/articles/9785956686349-Logos-Platform-Comparison)
- Online notes/highlights and reading activity sync across devices. Mobile can download books for offline reading and retains private notes/highlights and limited searching; full library search, Passage Guide, and Bible Word Study require internet. Desktop has substantially broader offline operation. Web requires internet. [FAQ](https://www.logos.com/faq?ssi=0), [Platform Comparison](https://support.logos.com/hc/en-us/articles/9785956686349-Logos-Platform-Comparison)
- Resource access differs by purchased library and subscription. Tools do not imply that all modern translations, study Bibles, commentaries, dictionaries, or language datasets are included. Logos explicitly ties guide output to subscription/legacy-package level and books purchased. [Passage Guide](https://support.logos.com/hc/en-us/articles/360016462872-Study-a-Passage-Using-the-Passage-Guide), [Bible Word Study](https://support.logos.com/hc/en-us/articles/360016688811-Studying-Words-Using-the-Bible-Word-Study-Guide)
- Subscriptions offer Smart Search, search-result summaries, Study Assistant conversations, article/chapter summarization, and Insights. Sermon Assistant is Pro/Max. These AI services are online and metered through monthly credits; Premium/Pro/Max have differing allowances. The Smart Search article also documents limited monthly access for non-subscribers. [Subscription Features](https://support.logos.com/hc/en-us/articles/21081346488205-What-s-included-in-Logos-subscriptions), [AI Credits](https://support.logos.com/hc/en-us/articles/23563051328269-About-AI-Credits), [Smart Search](https://support.logos.com/hc/en-us/articles/23526184005261-Find-Answers-Faster-with-Smart-Search)

## Documentation conflicts and limits

The platform matrix's reading-plan footnote says smartphones/web cannot create plans. The dedicated Reading Plans page explicitly documents creation on mobile/web, standard versus custom plan options, and mobile reminders. Prefer the feature-specific instructions for a current capabilities inventory, but validate the installed app version before promising a particular mobile workflow. Similarly, the matrix's interlinear footnote and dedicated mobile view/word-study documentation differ about how much interlinear information is displayed; safely claim original-word lookup on mobile, not complete desktop interlinear parity. [Platform Comparison](https://support.logos.com/hc/en-us/articles/9785956686349-Logos-Platform-Comparison), [Reading Plans](https://support.logos.com/hc/en-us/articles/360016525132-Work-Through-Your-Books-with-Reading-Plans), [Bible Word Study](https://support.logos.com/hc/en-us/articles/360016688811-Studying-Words-Using-the-Bible-Word-Study-Guide)

## Implications for a Cross Canon comparison

These are product judgments to test against the local feature inventory, not claims made by Logos:

1. The strongest daily-use baseline is continuing yesterday's reading, storing highlights/notes, keeping a reading plan, finding exact words, comparing a second translation, and quickly opening context/cross-references.
2. Treat annotation and saved passage collections as distinct from transient verse selection or search-result highlighting. Persistence and later retrieval create the daily-use value.
3. A lightweight plan and simple annotations are plausible near-term gaps; replicating Logos's entire licensed library or academic apparatus is a different product strategy.
4. Cross Canon can differentiate through an unobtrusive reader and easy source-based discovery across Scripture, patristics, and classics. Whether it does this better is a UX hypothesis, not something source documentation establishes.
5. Generated answers, AI summaries, sermon-writing tools, and specialized academic analyses should be considered strategic choices, not automatic parity requirements—especially given Cross Canon's explicit source-only search policy.
