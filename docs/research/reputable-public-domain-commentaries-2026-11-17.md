# Reputable public-domain commentaries: further candidates

Researched 2026-11-17. Extends [the earlier candidates](public-domain-library-candidates.md) and [import-source audit](christian-classics-import-sources.md). Research only: no imports, database updates, or application changes.

**Recommended additions:** Jamieson–Fausset–Brown, Calvin's CTS commentaries, Keil–Delitzsch, Spurgeon's *Treasury of David*, Matthew Poole, and J. C. Ryle. Haydock would add valuable Catholic breadth. The priorities below are editorial judgments about Cross Canon's collection; reputation evidence and source limitations are stated separately.

“Public domain” below describes the named historical text and the source's explicit digital rights declaration. It is **not a worldwide clearance**. Gutenberg explicitly limits its determination to the USA; [CCEL likewise warns that countries differ](https://www.ccel.org/about/copyright.html). Modern translations, introductions, abridgments, and revised apparatus require their own review.

## Best candidates

| Work and reader value | Verified edition, digital rights, and formats | Reputation evidence and remaining work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Jamieson, Fausset & Brown, *Commentary Critical and Explanatory on the Whole Bible***. Compact historical exposition; strongest immediate general-commentary addition. | **1871 one-volume** work. [Electronic editor Ernie Stefanik](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/jfb/jfb/JFB00H.htm) explains Woodside/OnLine Bible scanning, expanded abbreviations, corrections, and explicit PD permission. [CCEL raw ThML XML](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/j/jamieson/jfb.xml) independently says `DC.Rights: Public Domain`, print basis 1871. [Catalog](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/jamieson/jfb.html) offers HTML, text, XML, EPUB and PDF; prefer XML. [CrossWire JFB](https://www.crosswire.org/sword/modules/ModInfo.jsp?modName=JFB) is another explicitly PD package. | CCEL's institutional description commends the work's scholarly/devotional usefulness. Spurgeon's historical praise concerns the authors' **six-volume *Critical, Experimental, and Practical*** commentary, not this exact one-volume electronic edition. Label edition accurately; audit actual book/chapter coverage before calling an import complete. |
| **John Calvin, *Commentaries*, Calvin Translation Society edition**. Substantial Reformed exposition, with historical translators and editorial notes. | [CCEL CTS collection](https://ccel.org/c/calvin/comment3/comm_index.htm) explicitly releases its electronic files for any purpose; linked volume HTML and HTML downloads. The source is an English collection, not Calvin's original Latin or a modern anthology. [CrossWire CalvinCommentaries](https://www.crosswire.org/sword/modules/ModInfo.jsp?modName=CalvinCommentaries) v1.1 also explicitly PD. | [Spurgeon's own assessment](https://ccel.org/ccel/spurgeon/comment/files/comment.html) praises Calvin's fairness and specifically values CTS editorial additions. **Not the whole Bible:** the CTS index includes Acts, but the CrossWire catalog's listed books omit Acts. Ezekiel stops at chapter 20. Pin translators/print dates per volume and audit harmonized Gospel/Law passages; do not silently equate these collections. |
| **C. F. Keil & Franz Delitzsch, *Biblical Commentary on the Old Testament***. Historical grammatical/philological depth for Hebrew Bible study. | [CrossWire KD](https://www.crosswire.org/sword/modules/ModInfo.jsp?modName=KD), English v2.0, explicitly PD, downloadable SWORD module. It identifies the historical German/English series, BibleWorks/Willem Swanepoel electronic provenance, and a historical facsimile. | Institutional adoption is documented by [Cape Town Baptist Seminary's hosted commentary resources](https://www.ctbs.org.za/index.php/resources). This supports continued use, not universal scholarly endorsement. Catalog metadata does **not** establish the precise English translator/revision for every volume; inspect the source against historical title pages before assigning exact edition metadata. Historical scholarship, not a current critical commentary. |
| **C. H. Spurgeon, *The Treasury of David***. Psalms exposition, devotional reflection, quotations and preaching helps. | [CrossWire TDavid](https://www.crosswire.org/sword/modules/ModInfo.jsp?modName=TDavid), English v2.1, explicitly PD SWORD download. Identifies the original seven-volume work, final volume **1885**. | [The Spurgeon Archive](https://archive.spurgeon.org/treasury/treasury.php) documents its publication history, long reception, and Eric Hayden's favorable assessment. Strong specialized addition; preserve headings and attributed extracts, distinguish Spurgeon's prose from quoted commentators, and check all 150 Psalms. Do not substitute a modern abridgment. |
| **Matthew Poole, *Annotations upon the Holy Bible***. Concise English Puritan exposition; useful alongside Henry. | [StudyLight's Poole files](https://www.studylight.org/commentaries/eng/mpc/acts-1.html) explicitly identify files as PD, with BibleSupport provenance and historical bibliography. HTML available; exact digital edition and bulk retrieval still need an audit. | [Spurgeon's primary historical review](https://ccel.org/ccel/spurgeon/comment/files/comment.html) recommends the English *Annotations* after Henry. Distinguish it from Poole's **Latin *Synopsis Criticorum***. Poole's own work ends at Isaiah 58; continuators finish the commentary. Credit them rather than attributing every note solely to Poole. |
| **J. C. Ryle, *Expository Thoughts on the Gospels***. Accessible evangelical Anglican passage exposition. | **Matthew specifically verified:** [CCEL XML](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/r/ryle/matthew.xml) identifies **New York: Robert Carter, 1860** and explicitly says `Public Domain`. [Catalog](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/ryle/matthew.html) supplies HTML, ASCII, XML, EPUB and PDF. | [Banner of Truth's continued edition](https://banneroftruth.org/us/store/commentaries/expository-thoughts-on-the-gospels-9/) provides primary publisher evidence of reception. Raw Matthew XML has 28 chapter divisions and **93** commentary passage markers. Remaining Gospel volumes require independent edition, rights and coverage checks; do not describe this verified single volume as a complete seven-volume source. |
| **George Leo Haydock and collaborators, Catholic Bible commentary**. Adds Catholic exposition and deuterocanonical coverage beyond Catena's Gospels. | [eCatholic2000 title page](https://www.ecatholic2000.com/haydock/title.shtml) identifies **New York: Edward Dunigan & Brother, 1859**. [StudyLight Genesis](https://www.studylight.org/commentaries/eng/hcc/genesis-1.html) explicitly marks its files PD and exposes deuterocanonical navigation. HTML. | Good collection-balance choice, but edition identity must be checked across hosts. [SermonIndex](https://www.sermonindex.net/commentary/haydock-c/) explicitly permits copying/building and offers several Bible-software formats, yet claims deuterocanonical inclusion while displaying **66 books / 1,189 entries**. Resolve that contradiction and Vulgate numbering before import. An original title-page scan is evidence of provenance, not by itself evidence of electronic-file permission. |

## Further worthwhile options

- **Adam Clarke, *Commentary and Critical Notes***: Methodist breadth beyond Wesley. [CrossWire Clarke](https://www.crosswire.org/sword/modules/ModInfo.jsp?modName=Clarke) v2.0 explicitly PD, downloadable SWORD. Spurgeon ranks Clarke highly while criticizing his digressions and doctrinal bias. The module catalog does not pin a printed edition: audit before supplying publication metadata.
- **John Gill, *Exposition of the Old and New Testaments***: detailed Baptist/Reformed interpretation. Spurgeon praises its substance but explicitly criticizes forced doctrinal readings. [Sacred Texts' hosted edition](https://sacred-texts.com/book/exposition-of-the-old-and-new-testament) labels the work PD and provides HTML. Exact underlying print edition, digital provenance, and bulk structure remain less well established than JFB/Calvin; keep behind the better-audited candidates.
- **Albert Barnes, *New Testament Notes***: accessible explanations and cross references. [CrossWire Barnes](https://www.crosswire.org/sword/modules/ModInfo.jsp?modName=Barnes) explicitly PD, **NT only**. [CCEL XML](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/b/barnes/ntnotes.xml) also explicitly PD, but names **Baker Book House, 1949** as its print basis. This is a reprint/provenance question, not evidence that every component of any 1949 edition is automatically PD. Spurgeon's reception was mixed. Preserve the source grant and investigate added material.

The historical assessments above are from [Spurgeon's own *Commenting and Commentaries*](https://ccel.org/ccel/spurgeon/comment/files/comment.html); they represent one influential nineteenth-century Protestant reader, not a neutral cross-tradition consensus. His reviews supply historical reception evidence, not present-day accuracy guarantees.

## Broader, scan-heavy shortlist

- **Ellicott, *A New Testament Commentary for English Readers***: [hosted third edition, London: Cassell, Petter & Galpin, 1877–79](https://biblicalstudies.gospelstudies.org.uk/book_commentary-on-the-nt_ellicott.html), three PDF volumes, host explicitly PD. [Logos' continued eight-volume Bible edition](https://www.logos.com/product/8498/a-bible-commentary-for-english-readers-vols-i-viii) is reception evidence, not the same verified digital set.
- **The Pulpit Commentary**, Spence/Exell: [host](https://biblicalstudies.gospelstudies.org.uk/commentaries_pulpit-commentary.php) explicitly PD; individual PDFs and a large full-set ZIP. Useful breadth, heavier OCR/volume-edition audit. [Olive Tree's current edition](https://www.olivetree.com/store/product/87173) documents continued publication.
- **Nicoll's historical *Expositor's Bible***: Gutenberg's [Plummer, *Pastoral Epistles*, #39628](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/39628) and [Milligan, *Revelation*, #42251](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/42251) explicitly U.S. PD, with HTML/plain text/EPUB. Evaluate individual authors; this is not the modern *Expositor's Bible Commentary*.
- **Cornelius à Lapide, historical English *Great Commentary***: [Princeton-supplied volume 1 scan](https://archive.org/details/greatcommentarycalo01lapi), cataloged 1876, is a promising Mossman-era source. Verify all English volumes/translators separately; do not advertise Latin whole-Bible scope for an English Gospel subset. Exclude lapide.org's experimental AI translation from a shortlist of established historical English editions.

## Source audit and implementation implications

Downloaded raw CCEL XML for Ryle Matthew, JFB and Barnes NT and checked their metadata and strict XML parsing. All three parsed successfully. JFB is approximately **35.3 MB**, Barnes **32.3 MB**, Ryle **0.76 MB**. These are off-server conversion sources, not suitable to send wholesale to every reading client. Counts above describe markup, not a complete textual collation.

For any future import, preserve author/tradition, exact edition/translator, source URL, rights evidence and retrieval hash. Audit passage **ranges**, introductions, quotations and references separately: a citation inside a note does not identify the passage being explained. Check chapter/verse coverage independently, especially harmonies, missing books and Catholic numbering. Existing Henry/Wesley/Catena adapters and their documented anomalies are useful patterns, not proof that another source shares their structure.
